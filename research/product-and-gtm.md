# Product and GTM research — Mr Laziz

Researched 28 October 2026. Product recommendations below are hypotheses, not customer validation. Scope updated for Teo's explicit broad vision: a personal **and** business financial decision/action agent, including a phone purchase, a home purchase, and opening or growing a covrigărie. Two people are currently active; no existing application code; approximately 24 hours to submission. No application code was written for this research.

## Sell the outcome first

**Working name: Next Move. Promise: “Make your next money move with a plan you can afford—and help carrying it out.”** Name availability and trademarks have not been checked.

Customer explanation: “Tell us what you want to do: buy a phone, buy a home, open a pretzel shop, or take a large order. Next Move checks what happens to your money, compares realistic alternatives, and prepares the next steps for your approval. When a price, payment date, or offer changes, it updates the plan.”

One question anchors the whole product: **“What is the best workable way to do this, given my money, commitments, and priorities?”** The answer may be buy now, change the terms, choose a cheaper option, wait, collect money first, explore financing, or do not proceed under the stated assumptions. It should explain the tradeoff rather than optimize a hidden universal financial score.

The product stays broad. The implementation shares a single loop: understand the goal → obtain missing facts → calculate baseline and alternatives → propose a plan → prepare approved actions → incorporate replies → calculate again. Different situations activate different tools. A homebuyer does not need invoice chasing; a retailer does. Breadth means the same reliable process works across contexts, not that every feature appears on every screen.

## Combine the original five concepts by relevance

| Original concept | Role in the shared product | Where it belongs |
|---|---|---|
| Growth/order decisions and payment terms | Compare taking an opportunity with doing nothing, changing scope, or negotiating terms | Business opportunities and a shop-opening plan |
| Cash-aware replenishment | Schedule the stock actually needed, using stock levels, demand assumptions, lead times, and cash availability | Product businesses; absent from personal purchase journeys |
| Stable owner/freelancer personal income | Protect personal commitments and model variable income conservatively | Personal decisions and owner-operated businesses |
| Invoice collection | Prepare a reminder or payment-plan proposal; update expected receipts after a reply | Businesses/freelancers with receivables |
| Product comparison and onboarding preparation | Compare relevant products against the remaining need; assemble questions and an application checklist | A purchase, a mortgage conversation, or business working capital |

**Critical boundary:** five separate home screens, five agents, and five unrelated promises would dilute both clarity and delivery. Keep one goal input and one decision workspace. A fully general agent is the product direction; tomorrow's reproducible support is three explicit scenario families. Label unsupported cases and missing inputs instead of pretending universal financial competence.

## Who pays, without restricting who the product serves?

Supported audience: individuals, households, freelancers, aspiring business owners, and operating businesses. There is no evidence yet that one pricing model will serve all of them.

An initial **paying-customer hypothesis**, not a product restriction: an owner of a small Romanian business who personally decides both household commitments and business purchases, faces at least one material cash decision a month, and currently assembles facts from spreadsheets, invoices, and bank balances. A product business selling online and through B2B orders is a strong first *business test case* because stock, payment terms, collections, owner income, and financing can interact naturally. It is a weak definition of the entire product because it excludes Teo's phone/home/startup examples.

Run a parallel personal test cohort: people with a real purchase or home decision in the next 90 days. Their needs may be episodic, so a one-time paid decision plan may fit better than a subscription. Neither cohort is validated. Pick distribution sequences based on access and observed willingness to pay, not a claim that other users are out of scope.

### Separate household and business money

Use separate ledgers, balances, commitments, and reserve floors. The owner sets a desired personal income; the business forecast includes only explicitly confirmed transfers through an already established mechanism. The personal forecast records the matching incoming transfer once, never as money newly created. Do not assume company cash is personally spendable or invent salary/dividend/tax rules. If a proposed transfer is not confirmed, show the personal budget as conditional and ask for the missing fact.

For variable income, show committed receipts separately from hoped-for receipts and a stress scenario. Do not promise “stable income” when the forecast cannot support it. The product value is making the constraint visible and offering alternatives.

### A defensible proposed difference

**A traceable decision-to-action loop across personal and business contexts:** compare the same goal against a dated baseline; show which assumptions make each plan feasible; prepare specific actions; revise after a reply; preserve personal/business separation; show the resulting incremental cash and the remaining conditions.

This is a positioning hypothesis, **not an exclusivity claim or an established moat**. Several competitors may implement parts of it. Tomorrow's claim should be: “We demonstrate this loop in these three cases.” A longer-term advantage would have to come from successful local workflows, reliable tool integrations, outcome evidence, and distribution. Calling it “the first AI CFO” is unsupported.

An English judge-facing line: **“Advice tells you what might work. We show the plan, the conditions it depends on, and what changes when reality replies.”** Use it to describe the prototype, not to misrepresent competitors as advice-only.

### Checked synthetic business fixture

All amounts below are invented cash entries in RON for a closed teaching scenario, not a Romanian tax template, live offer, or revenue forecast. All other modeled cash entries are zero; do not portray this as a complete real business forecast. Use Day 0–30 rather than calendar dates. Track intraday event order if events share a date.

Baseline: opening business cash **26,000**; confirmed owner transfer **−4,000 on day 3**; other commitments **−10,000 on day 7**; existing invoice **+12,000 on day 12**; online receipts **+10,000 on day 15**. Business floor **8,000**. Baseline minimum **12,000** and closing cash **34,000**.

Opportunity: additional replenishment **−24,000 on day 2**, additional customer receipts **+40,000 on day 30**. Assume confirmed stock/capacity, supplier lead time, and all incremental cash costs are captured. Accepting unchanged reaches **−12,000**, closing **50,000**. The order contributes **16,000** before financing costs within this fixture; positive contribution does not solve the timing gap.

First proposed plan: customer deposit **12,000 on day 1**, remaining **28,000 on day 30**, and collect the existing **12,000 on day 5** instead of day 12. Minimum becomes **10,000**; closing remains **50,000**. Neither negotiated deposit nor collection is treated as confirmed before a reply.

Reply: existing debtor can pay only **4,000 on day 5**, with **8,000 on day 12**. Updated minimum is **4,000**, so the floor is missed by **4,000**. The agent must not keep recommending the original plan.

Mock offer A: conditional short facility, draw **4,200 day 6**, repay principal **4,200 plus total modeled fee 100 day 12**; hypothetical funding availability explicitly stated. Mock offer B: same amount/dates with **350** modeled total fee. Any unmodeled collateral/eligibility condition is “unknown,” not “approved.” Offer A yields minimum **8,200**, day-30 **49,900**, delta versus baseline **+15,900**. The extra 200 is a stated tiny buffer in this teaching fixture, not a recommended real-world cushion. The plan only meets the floor under its assumptions.

If funding is unavailable or a deposit is refused, return “not feasible under these terms” and investigate supplier staging, a smaller order, a later delivery, or rejection. Do not invent a bank approval to force success. Accelerated invoice cash and loan principal are **not new revenue**; show timing changes separately from incremental order cash.

### Two additional reproducible cases

**Phone:** synthetic available personal cash 12,000; rent 3,000 before next payday; protected floor 5,000. A 4,500 phone leaves only 4,500 after rent and breaches the floor. A 3,200 alternative leaves 5,800; waiting until a confirmed 6,000 payday also changes feasibility. Compare total quoted cost and timing, not just installment size. Prepare a saved comparison/purchase checklist; no checkout necessary. If next income is uncertain, make the waiting result conditional.

**Home:** synthetic cash 120,000; chosen deposit 100,000; user-provided transaction allowance 10,000; moving allowance 5,000; desired remaining reserve 30,000. Required upfront funds 145,000; gap **25,000**. At an explicitly supplied 5,000 monthly savings capacity, earliest cash-only readiness is **five months**, if all values stay unchanged. Separately test the household budget using a supplied/mock loan payment; do not derive credit eligibility from cash readiness. The prototype prepares missing-quote questions and an onboarding packet. A delay or “need more information” is a complete decision outcome, not a failed demo.

**Opening a covrigărie:** supported as a planning template sharing setup-cost, working-capital, demand, supplier, and personal-reserve tools. Do not pretend a new shop already has receivables. Use the existing-shop catering order for the deep five-function demo, and label new-shop full workflows as stretch unless actually tested. New-shop projections require user-supplied assumptions, including any approvals/costs they know; the agent does not invent licensing rules.

## Tomorrow's MVP and extensions

### Must be real in the prototype

1. Shared goal/profile structure and dated event ledger; distinct household/business ownership.
2. Three loadable synthetic fixtures: business order, phone, home. One shared calculation path; editable amounts/date; explicit missing-input state.
3. Baseline vs at least two alternatives; minimum cash, floor breach date, ending delta, total modeled financing cost, and assumptions.
4. One real model-driven tool loop that obtains records, calls calculations, prepares actions, observes a simulated reply, and calls calculations again. Do not prewrite every answer or silently fall back to a fixed tree.
5. One business reply changes the recommendation. State persisted through a refresh; approvals tied to a specific current plan version.
6. A mock product catalog with source label, conditions, availability assumption, and unknowns; comparison and bank-preparation packet. No live bank connection required for this demonstration.
7. Downloadable/local decision packet and sandbox action receipts; visible “synthetic / simulated / conditional” labels.

### Stretch, chosen only after that loop works

One sanctioned read-only CSV import; one real data adapter; a fuller shop-opening template; uncertainty ranges; a realistic personal variable-income history; an optional read-only live product lookup with source dates. Live bank integrations, autonomous payments, real loan origination, production inbox management, tax computations, and broad inventory forecasting are later product work. This preserves the broad engine while being precise about tomorrow's evidence.

### Architecture: one agent, five tool surfaces

- **Read context:** profile, records, ownership, source dates, and commitments from fixtures/approved imports.
- **Simulate plan:** deterministic dated cash arithmetic, stock/capacity constraints when relevant, and baseline comparison. The model does not invent the answer's arithmetic.
- **Compare options:** retrieve relevant mock offers/products and surface missing fields; context selects the catalog.
- **Prepare action:** create a proposed message, checklist, supplier request, or bank packet with concrete amounts/dates and recipients where relevant.
- **Record approved action/event:** apply a sandbox action or ingest a counterparty reply; update state; invalidate outdated approvals; trigger replanning.

The UI can have three areas: goal/context, compared plans, action timeline. Avoid multiple pretend specialists. Important states: draft → awaiting approval → simulated execution → awaiting reply → revised plan. An unsent draft is reversible; an already sent real message or payment is not. Never describe all actions as reversible. Approve exact action contents; repeat clicks must not create duplicate sandbox actions; a changed cash date must invalidate approval of the old plan.

**Proportionate acceptance cases:** all three fixtures calculate correctly; a late/partial invoice changes the plan; unknown mortgage costs trigger questions; unavailable funding prevents unconditional acceptance; household and business transfer is counted once on each correct ledger; stale/repeated approval cannot execute the wrong/duplicate action. Show calculation evidence separately from model wording.

## 30/60/90-day GTM hypotheses

The broad supported audience remains the same. Cohorts below are a sequence for learning and distribution, not a replacement product scope.

**Days 1–30: prove decision value.** Recruit 10 personal decision-makers and 10 owner/freelancer decision-makers through founder contacts, relevant local communities with permission, accountants, and mentor introductions. Require a specific real decision due within 90 days; manually onboard with consented inputs. Offer a paid decision session or short pilot, not a free waitlist alone. Measure completion, facts supplied, recommendation changed by evidence, actions actually taken, and willingness to pay. Target hypotheses: at least 12/20 can complete a useful plan, at least 6 buy a follow-up/pilot, and no unresolved material arithmetic errors. These are proposed gates, not observed conversion rates.

**Days 31–60: test two payment shapes.** Personal: test **€9–29 per decision** or **€5–12/month** only where monitoring is useful. Business: test **€39–99/month** for recurring plans/actions, with a separate clearly priced onboarding service if needed. A secondary channel experiment is 2–3 accountants/bookkeepers or founder/community advisers introducing clients; begin with referrals, not a complex reseller platform. Track paid retention and support time by cohort. No bank partnership is needed to sell a planning tool with user-provided information and prepared action drafts; any future regulated/connected services need separate scoping.

**Days 61–90: deepen what converts, keep the engine broad.** Improve the two or three workflows showing paid repeat use, add one integration selected by actual input friction, and test one partner referral funnel. Keep phone/home/business starter paths visible with explicit capability labels. Only approach a bank for distribution/integration after showing actual plan completion and action outcomes; do not make a bank deal the path to the first paying user.

For pricing context, Float currently advertises a smaller-business entry from **£99/month** on its official pricing page; Chaser's pricing lists Europe Compact from **€239/month** with monthly selection. These are different scopes and markets, not proof Teo's product can charge similar prices. [Float pricing](https://www.floatapp.com/pricing), [Chaser pricing](https://www.chaserhq.com/chaser-pricing). ### Falsifiable interview questions

Ask about behavior before pitching:

- “What was your last decision involving money that you delayed or regretted? Show me how you worked it out.”
- “Which figures did you not know, and where did you get them? How long did that take?”
- “What changed after you made the plan: price, pay date, customer reply, interest quote? What did you do then?”
- “What could this agent prepare that you would actually approve? Which action would you never delegate?”
- “Can you provide these specific inputs now, and pay €X for help with the real decision you just described?”
- For business owners: “When did business cash last affect your planned personal income, or a personal commitment affect a business decision?”
- After a prototype session: “What would you do differently because of this plan? What would you still need to verify?”

Count a payment or an agreed paid pilot separately from a compliment. Do not call mentor enthusiasm customer validation.

### Kill or pivot gates

- If fewer than 5 of 20 qualified interviews reveal a concrete recurring or high-consequence problem, revisit the promise and workflow mix before adding integrations.
- If users will not provide minimum facts even through assisted onboarding, test a smaller input requirement; if completion remains below 50%, pause self-serve expansion.
- If fewer than 3 of 15 completed useful sessions yield any purchase at tested prices, revisit pricing, buyer, and distribution. Do not infer market demand from demo applause.
- If personal usage is episodic but paid, use one-off decision pricing rather than forcing a subscription. If business use repeats, subscription can coexist in the same product.
- If outputs never change a user's next action versus their existing spreadsheet, sharpen the action/replanning value or pivot the workflow.
- Any unexplained material calculation error or recommendation based on fabricated eligibility blocks releasing that workflow until corrected. This is a quality gate, not evidence that the broad vision must shrink.
