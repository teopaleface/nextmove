## 3. GDPR: purpose-specific processing, not blanket consent

**Data boundary.** Replacing names or encrypting a real bank export does not make it anonymous. A real Screen Time image can identify a person and reveal other people. IP addresses, account/session links and human support records also need assessment. “Demo” is not an exemption for these flows. [GDPR Articles 2–4 and Recital 26; Commission explanation](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/application-gdpr_en).

**Roles — interpretation.** A bank generally determines its banking purposes; NextMove could be its instructed processor for specified operations while controlling separate product purposes. An independent consumer service may itself be controller. A model vendor is not automatically a processor: its own training or other purposes can change the analysis. Map each flow, including joint determination; use Articles 26/28 arrangements where appropriate. Contract labels alone do not settle the roles. [EDPB Guidelines 07/2020, Part I Sections 2–4 and Part II](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf).

**Lawful bases — interpretation.** Map each purpose to Article 6 before collection. Contract necessity, Article 6(1)(b), may support information objectively needed for a user-requested comparison/cancellation service; it does not automatically cover continuous profiling, analytics or model improvement. Legal obligation, Article 6(1)(c), needs an actual applicable obligation. Security processing may qualify under Article 6(1)(f) after necessity/balancing assessment. Where consent is chosen, Articles 4(11)/7 require genuine choice, specificity and easy withdrawal; accepting general terms is insufficient. [EDPB Guidelines 2/2019, paragraphs 24–33, 48–50](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines-art_6-1-b-adopted_after_public_consultation_en.pdf); [Commission lawful-basis/consent guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en).

Keep **GDPR basis**, **PSD2 access consent**, **monitoring permission** and **authority for an exact contractual action** distinct. PSD2 “explicit consent” is not automatically GDPR consent. Counterparty data and transactions revealing health, religion or other special categories require separate analysis; Article 6 alone does not resolve Article 9. Minimise such data and avoid unnecessary sensitive inferences. [EDPB Guidelines 06/2020, Sections 3–5](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202006_psd2_afterpublicconsultation_en.pdf).

**DPIA and Article 22 — interpretation.** Screen the real design before processing against Article 35 and ANSPDCP Decision 174/2018, Article 1(1), especially systematic significant-effect profiling, sensitive-data scale, vulnerable people and innovative large-scale processing. Complete a DPIA where required; unresolved high residual risk can require prior consultation under Article 36. “200 subscriptions” alone is not a statutory large-scale threshold. [ANSPDCP decision](https://www.dataprotection.ro/servlet/ViewDocument?id=1556); [EDPB endorsed DPIA guidance, WP248 rev.01](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/endorsed-wp29-guidelines_en).

Article 22 concerns **solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects**, not every recommendation. Its contract-necessity, legislative-authorisation and explicit-consent exceptions have conditions; relevant safeguards include intervention, expressing a view and contesting a decision. A user's informed selection of their own cancellation differs from automated bank credit rejection, but a confirmation button or rubber-stamp reviewer does not by itself resolve Article 22. Document who can understand, change and reverse each decision. [GDPR Article 22; EDPB rights guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en).

Required privacy evidence, currently **unverified**:

- A named vendor/subprocessor and data-flow register covering model/vision, hosting, browser workers, telemetry, backups and support. Contractually establish instructions, reuse/training restrictions, deletion and assistance; do not claim “no training” or “EU-only” without evidence.
- For each non-EEA recipient/access route, document the Chapter V mechanism: applicable adequacy decision or appropriate safeguards, such as SCCs with transfer assessment/supplementary measures where needed. An EU server location alone does not settle recipient/support access. [GDPR Articles 44–49; EDPB transfer guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/international-data-transfers_en).
- A category-specific retention/deletion schedule for raw images, extracted facts, prompts, action receipts, logs and backups, including justified legal holds. No universal EU “30-day” period is established here. Provide Articles 12–22 rights handling and Articles 13/14 notices. [GDPR Article 5(1)(c), (e); Commission principles and retention guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en).
- Risk-based security, incident ownership and breach procedures. Article 33 requires controller notification without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of awareness unless unlikely to create risk; processors notify controllers without undue delay. Article 34's separate high-risk communication test and exceptions need assessment. DPO appointment is conditional under Article 37. [GDPR Articles 25, 32–37](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

## 4. AI Act: roles and financial intended purpose

**Role — interpretation.** Using a third-party GPAI API does not make NextMove the provider of that underlying model. The developer marketing its own AI system can nevertheless be an **AI-system provider**, with the bank as deployer; operating its own system can combine roles. Rebranding, substantial changes or changed intended purpose need reassessment under Articles 3 and 25. Chapter V GPAI-provider duties, including Article 53, are distinct from system/deployer duties. [AI Act Articles 3, 25, 53](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689); [Commission GPAI-provider guidance and transition dates](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers).

A laboratory prototype may fall within Article 2(8)'s pre-market research/testing/development exclusion. That exclusion expressly excludes real-world testing. A hackathon label, fictional transactions or an upstream provider's compliance does not automatically establish NextMove's exemption. [Commission reproduction of Article 2(6)–(8)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2).

**High risk — interpretation.** Budgeting and ordinary subscription comparison are not high-risk merely because shown in a banking app. Evaluating natural-person creditworthiness or establishing credit scores falls within Annex III **5(b)**, apart from its financial-fraud exception. Human review does not automatically remove classification; Article 6(3)'s exceptions are conditional and Annex III profiling remains high-risk. Business-seat optimisation must not silently become employee performance monitoring, potentially Annex III **4(b)**. [Annex III](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3); [Article 6(2)–(4)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-6).

For a future creditworthiness feature, plan the applicable provider controls in Articles 9–15, deployer duties in Article 26 and the Article 27(1) fundamental-rights assessment for Annex III 5(b) deployers, alongside any GDPR DPIA. Their relevant revised application date is **2 December 2027**, not a present certification finding. [AI Act Articles 26–27](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689).

**Transparency.** Implement clear AI identification at the first interaction under Article 50(1)/(5). Assess Article 50(2)'s machine-readable marking/detectability duty for generated outputs and its technical conditions separately; a visible “AI” label alone does not satisfy that separate duty. Article 50(4)'s deployer disclosure rules concern deepfakes and public-interest text publication, with their own exceptions, rather than every private financial reply. [Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50). Keep current Article 4 literacy support and prohibited-practice assessment in the design; do not describe the July amendment as removing all literacy responsibility.

## 5. Payments and bank integration

**Interpretation:** the selected bank-partner route requires a genuine bank-controlled allocation of functions and responsibility. It differs from NextMove independently providing account aggregation or payment initiation. Embedding the interface alone does not settle the perimeter. PSD2 Article 3(j)'s technical-service exclusion explicitly excludes AISP/PISP services; never assume “we do not hold funds” is sufficient. [PSD2 Articles 3(j), 4 and Annex I(7)–(8)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/2366/oj).

A standalone AISP normally follows Article 33's registration regime; PISP activity requires the appropriate authorisation/permissions, not AISP registration alone. Verify the actual entity, activities and Romanian/EEA passport coverage in the national register; the EBA register is a useful cross-check but expressly has no constitutive legal effect. Romania's framework includes Law 209/2019 and BNR Regulation 4/2019, subsequently amended; this report is not a current consolidated licensing opinion. [EBA register and national-register links](https://www.eba.europa.eu/risk-and-data-analysis/data/registers/payment-institutions-register); [BNR report describing Regulation 4/2019 and its 2021 amendment, Section 2.3](https://www.bnr.ro/files/d/Pubs_ro/Anuale/RAPORT%20ANUAL%202021.pdf).

Article 97 requires SCA in defined circumstances, with transaction dynamic linking for relevant remote payments; lawful exemptions are governed by the RTS. A NextMove approval card is not bank SCA. [PSD2 Article 97](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/2366/oj); [Regulation 2018/389, Articles 2–9 and Chapter III](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2018/389/oj).

**Product requirement:** no real banking password, PIN, OTP or reusable credential enters the model, prompt, screenshots, logs or agent browser. Use a contracted regulated provider's secure bank authentication/token flow and scoped revocable access. This architecture remains unimplemented; it is not a claim that EU law mandates one particular OAuth product.

## 6. DORA and suppliers

DORA Article 2 covers listed financial entities, including banks, payment institutions and AISPs, and ICT third-party providers. A banking-looking app is not thereby a financial entity. For a bank supplier, Articles 28–30 govern the financial entity's ICT third-party risk and contractual arrangements; outsourcing leaves that entity responsible. Contract requirements include service/data locations, security/incident assistance, access/audit and exit provisions, with additional requirements for critical or important functions. [DORA Articles 2, 28–30](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj).

The separate direct EU oversight regime concerns ICT providers **designated critical** under Article 31, with oversight powers under Articles 33–44. Supplying software to one bank does not automatically impose every bank-level DORA duty on the supplier. Classification and contract flow-down need review. [EBA oversight guide, paragraphs 6–8](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-07/32044d65-455e-4fff-82d0-aa0d8ac2799f/JC%202025%2029%20%20DORA%20Oversight%20Guide.pdf). No bank contract, designation, resilience test or supplier approval has been verified for NextMove.

## 7. Cancellation, withdrawal and annual commitments

Keep three actions separate:

1. **Contract cancellation/non-renewal:** changes contractual obligations under the relevant right and effective date.
2. **Withdrawal of payment consent/mandate:** PSD2 Article 64(3) addresses consent for future payments, subject to Article 80 irrevocability. Direct-debit orders have the Article 80(3) cut-off; this is not a universal same-day stop rule.
3. **Card/payment blocking:** acts on the payment instrument/provider process. It does not itself establish termination of the underlying contract or erase an existing debt. This consequence is a contract-specific legal interpretation, not a card-control guarantee. [PSD2 Articles 64(3), 80](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/2366/oj).

The Consumer Rights Directive's usual distance-service withdrawal period is **14 days from contract conclusion**, subject to scope, information duties and exceptions. Fully performed paid services require the relevant prior express consent/acknowledgment conditions for loss of withdrawal rights; digital content not on a tangible medium has separate conditions. Starting any subscription does not automatically extinguish every withdrawal right. [Directive 2011/83/EU, Articles 9–10, 14(3)–(4), 16(a)/(m), current consolidation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02011L0083-20260927).

Romania's OUG 34/2014 addresses withdrawal in Articles 9–16. The ANPC-hosted copy checked is older and cannot establish all amendments applicable in October 2026. Financial services have specific rules; do not copy an ordinary streaming-contract analysis onto a bank/credit contract. [ANPC published text](https://anpc.ro/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Ordonanta-de-urgenta-34-2014-din-Lege-5.html).

The online withdrawal function introduced by Directive 2023/2673, Article 1(3), inserting Article 11a into 2011/83, operates **during an existing withdrawal period**. It does not create perpetual cancellation or restart 14 days on every renewal. Check Romanian implementing measures and sector-specific rules before claiming enforceability against a particular merchant. [Directive 2023/2673, Articles 1–3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2673/oj).

**Annual fees/penalties — interpretation.** Annual commitment billed monthly is not necessarily a monthly terminable plan. Show remaining term, notice, renewal, access/data loss, proportionate service charges where relevant, refund conditions and any early-termination demand. There is no blanket EU promise of penalty-free exit or automatic pro-rata annual refund. Conversely, a merchant's penalty is not automatically valid: Directive 93/13/EEC Articles 3–6 and Annex 1(e) address unfair terms/disproportionate compensation. Escalate disputed terms rather than accepting or ignoring them automatically. [Unfair Terms Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1993/13/oj).

**Evidence nuance:** statutory withdrawal can be a timely unilateral notice; Article 11(2)/(4) concerns dispatch and proof. A merchant's approval is not universally a legal prerequisite. Preserve `notice dispatched`, `merchant acknowledgment`, `contractual/legal effect disputed` and `payment reconciliation` separately. Do not let waiting for a reply miss a notice deadline, or let a merchant denial automatically override a statutory right. [Directive 2011/83/EU, Article 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02011L0083-20260927).

## 8. Investment, credit and business boundaries

**Interpretation:** general budgeting differs from a personal recommendation about transactions in financial instruments. The latter can be MiFID investment advice irrespective of an “information only” disclaimer. Assess authorisation, conflicts, costs and suitability before enabling it: MiFID II Articles **4(1)(4), 5, 24–25**, Annex I Section A(5). [MiFID II](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2014/65/oj); [ASF regulation, Article 127, investment-consultant authorisation](https://asfromania.ro/uploads/articole/attachments/60814b3eaf523879323106.pdf).

Personalised credit advice/intermediation needs a separate perimeter assessment. Residential mortgage advice/intermediaries are addressed by Directive 2014/17/EU Articles **22 and 29**; ANPC publishes a Romanian registration route under OUG 52/2016. [Mortgage Credit Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2014/17/oj); [ANPC intermediary guidance](https://www.anpc.ro/galerie/file/financiar/2017/Material_intermediari_de_credite.pdf). This historical guidance is not proof of NextMove's registration or current procedural completeness.

Prepare for CCD II Article **16** advisory-service rules and Article **18(8)** rights to explanation, a view and human review where creditworthiness uses automated personal-data processing, with national application from **20 November 2026**. Verify Romanian transposition and existing national law separately. [Directive 2023/2225, Articles 16, 18 and 48](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2225/oj).

Corporate cash forecasts, invoice collection, inventory and seat changes need organization authority and separate ledgers. Consumer rights do not automatically extend to a business purchase: see 2011/83 Article **2(1)**. Employee, owner, guarantor and sole-trader information can still be personal data. Corporate lending is not automatically natural-person credit scoring, but guarantees/personal assessment can change the analysis. Keep tax/payroll/legal classification and regulated lending outside automatic inference; assess each expansion before activation.

## 9. Operational requirements, including 200 cancellations

These are proposed engineering controls responding to the boundaries above, not legal safe harbours or completed tests:

1. **Enforce synthetic mode.** Isolated sessions, synthetic uploads/identities, allowlisted fake merchants, no production bank/merchant writes. Disclose fictional amounts and offers; prevent accidental real-data uploads or treat them under an approved real-data flow.
2. **Keep authority outside the model.** Server-side authentication/tenant and organization checks; typed allowlists; untrusted pages/documents cannot grant permission. Keep secrets and authentication challenges outside model-visible state. Test cross-session access and prompt-injection attempts.
3. **Bind approvals to exact items.** A batch may contain 200 identifiable actions, with merchant/account, operation, terms version, fees, effective date, lost benefits/data and expiry. Present a readable total and selectable item details. Approving a fixed manifest need not mean 200 separate dialogs, but “optimise everything forever” is not that approval. Changes invalidate the affected unexecuted grant; monitoring alone cannot commit.
4. **Persist and bound execution.** Queue per-item tasks; cap concurrency per user/merchant/provider, honour rate limits and deadlines, lease jobs, and serialize changes to the same subscription. Use stable action identities/idempotency where supported. After an ambiguous write, reconcile before retrying; do not promise universal exactly-once effects across real merchants.
5. **Make partial completion explicit.** Keep requested/pending/refused/unknown/confirmed outcomes and legal-notice evidence per item. Atomically order revocation against granting dispatch authority: no new dispatch grant after revocation, while already-authorised in-flight work can still reach the merchant. Stopping cannot undo completed changes. Do not automatically resubscribe as rollback. Escalate disputed fees, authority or authentication.
6. **Account from evidence.** Retain minimal approval/notice/receipt provenance. Update future charges only with a supported effect and date; preserve disputed obligations. A refund request is not credited money. Net savings include remaining liabilities, exit fees, replacement cost and overlapping periods; never multiply one-off trial savings by twelve without justification.
7. **Verify the workload before claiming scale.** Exercise a 200-item synthetic batch with mixed deadlines, changed terms, revocation, merchant refusal, worker restart and response loss after commit. Check isolation, duplicates, partial results and receipt reconciliation. This capacity has not been demonstrated by the planning documents or this research.

Before real-data/real-action enablement, obtain a qualified review of the concrete intended purposes and entity roles, current Romanian implementing law and licences/partner permissions; complete required impact assessments and vendor/transfer/retention evidence; then verify the implemented controls. This bounded review does not establish every launch obligation in AML/KYC, accessibility, national cybersecurity, employment or tax law. **No claim that NextMove is legally compliant, regulator-approved or certified is supported at this stage.**

## 10. Parent supplement: accessibility of a consumer-banking feature

The European Accessibility Act, Directive 2019/882, covers consumer banking services under Article 2(2)(d); national application generally starts **28 June 2025**, Article 31(2), with scope, exemptions and Article 32 transitional provisions requiring assessment. In Romania, Law **232/2022**, Article **2(3)(c)**, covers consumer banking services supplied after that date. Its Annex 1, point **4.1(e)**, requires accessible identification/security/payment functions and understandable information with complexity not above CEFR B2. [EU directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj), [official Romanian law](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/257778).

**Product interpretation:** treat accessible review of the complete batch, screen-reader labels/status updates, readable consequences, keyboard/switch navigation, text enlargement and the protected authentication path as acceptance requirements for the bank feature. A contrast check, 44px targets, a mockup or the choice of a WCAG target alone does not establish legal conformity. The bank must assess the exact service, applicable current standards, exceptions/transitions and required evidence. This supplement does not complete the broader launch-law assessment noted above.
